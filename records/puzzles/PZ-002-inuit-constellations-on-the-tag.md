## Selected opening mechanism - 2026-10-21
The designer chose the **cutout approach for the first puzzle**. Players use three
window pieces on a star chart, one each for the selected Inuit constellations (Q-005):
Aagjuuk, Tukturjuit and Ullaktut. Marked positions align with stars; each piece should
have exactly one correct placement. Players count the stars exposed in each window.
Order the counts by the uncle's own dated observations (Q-009), then open the envelope
bearing that answer directly (PR-008).

The mechanism is decided, but it has not been built or tested. Exact observation dates,
final code, chart, cutout geometry, alignment cues and print sizes remain open. The
observation-page format is PP-010. Do not infer that all three cutout pieces must share
its dimensions. The prototype chart/mask props are PP-011 and PP-012, available at the start.
PP-010 supplies the eventual dated observation note; that note is still unwritten.
PP-008 is the welcome card and is no longer the counting surface in this version.

## Current chart/cutout prototype - 2026-10-21
Requested first printable design is now PP-011 / PP-012, with player PDFs AS-011 and
AS-012, and designer-only review AS-013. Three independently enlarged catalogue-based
fields fit on one letter sheet. Each mask matches three visibly ringed reference stars;
its large window exposes the constellation stars to count. The prop and asset records
hold specifications and digital checks. Geometry is not a physical playtest.

The first arbitrary-star alignment attempt was ambiguous, leading to the visibly ringed
reference-star prototype. Current geometry passes unique-placement/count checks. This
specification is candidate for designer review; it is not a newly fixed art constraint.
The dated observation note and final code order remain open. The global kit still has
its old planet cipher configuration (PR-008); no labels are produced from it here.

## Prototype requirements
- One convincing correct placement per piece; verify this on the actual chart.
- Give special attention to Aagjuuk: matching just two stars may permit other placements.
- A main window should require counting chart stars. One separate hole per answer star
  risks making the chart unnecessary because players could count the holes.
- Determine front/back orientation, whether pieces can rotate, and readable alignment
  tolerance before printing. No final alignment design or numerical tolerance is chosen.
- Test the physical print and cut pieces. Status decided does not mean passed.

## Earlier lock mechanism - superseded format, retained for reference
The first lock of the game.

The front of the luggage tag (PP-008) shows the northern sky with three Inuit constellations,
each drawn in the colour of one wheel of the 3-digit colour lock. The number of stars in
each colour gives that wheel's digit. The colours set the order, so no instructions are needed.

Opens a locked compartment of the backpack (PP-009) holding the Galileo kit for PZ-001:
the sky map (PP-005), the magnifier (PP-006) and the uncle's note (PP-007).

Chronologically first: Inuit sky knowledge comes before Galileo in the Looking era (ST-001).

The three constellations (Q-005): Tukturjuit ("the caribou", Big Dipper, 7 stars),
Ullaktut ("the runners", Orion's Belt, 3 stars), Aagjuuk (Altair and Tarazed, 2 stars).
Digits are 7-3-2, in whichever order matches the lock's wheel colours.

Open: the lock's wheel colours (known once the lock is bought, Q-006). AS-002 draws the
tag front with placeholder colours (red, blue, green) until then.
The former back designation was superseded by the designer on 2026-10-05.
