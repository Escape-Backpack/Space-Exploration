A note from the uncle about Galileo, containing all four dated observations
used by PZ-001 and a hand-drawn lens/ray schematic. The designer selected this
note-only arrangement on 2026-10-05 (Q-007); the former split with PP-002 is
superseded. AS-009 is the illustrated draft. Exact wording and artwork remain
candidate for review; physical dimensions and print testing are not established.

## Note and optics direction (2026-10-05)
The designer proposed a handwritten introduction praising Galileo's telescope
achievements, followed by dated observations. A hand-drawn telescope optics
diagram should show lens magnification, with the text or drawing introducing
magnification naturally. Players receive the magnifying glass (PP-006).
Handwriting follows PR-007. The full draft below and AS-009 implement that
direction. The observation blocks are out of date order so chronology remains
part of the puzzle. No answer digits are printed on the note.

## Historical checks for drafting
- Prefer "a great advance in exploring the sky" over claiming Galileo made the
  first advance; earlier sky knowledge is part of this game's opening.
- Saturn: the checked source supports July 1610 and a three-bodied appearance,
  not Galileo identifying rings. The previous July 25 date is retained in Q-004
  as superseded, unverified day precision.
- Venus: October 1610 refers to the beginning of observations; Rice's timeline
  puts verification of the phases in December. The draft uses December 1610;
  Q-004 preserves the earlier October shorthand as superseded history.
- A Galilean telescope has a converging objective and a diverging eyepiece.
  A lens/ray sketch should use that arrangement if labelled as Galileo's telescope.

## Player sees

One of the great advances in exploring the sky came from Galileo. It is incredible what he achieved with his telescopes!

### Galileo's observations
- July 1610 - Saturn appeared to have two smaller bodies beside it.
- November 1609 - He saw mountains and craters on the Moon.
- December 1610 - He confirmed that Venus changes phases like the Moon.
- January 1610 - He discovered four moons orbiting Jupiter.

### How the telescope works
A hand-drawn Galilean telescope schematic with a convex objective, a concave
eyepiece and three light rays travelling toward an eye. Labels: "Light rays",
"Objective lens (convex)", "Eyepiece (concave)", "Eye".

Caption: **Magnification reveals small details.**

I love following his discoveries through time. A good lens can reveal details that are easy to miss.
